# Testing notes from the first workflow

## Key points

- A regulated firm has to be able to answer a supervisor's or auditor's questions about any AI output after the fact: which model version and prompt version produced it, what it said, who acted on it, where the client data went, how the feature was tested and who signed it off. A classic backend cannot answer these. The model belongs to a third party, can change behind an alias, may not give the same output twice (it picks words with randomness and can invent content) and receives client data on every call.
- FINMA Guidance 08/2024 (18 Dec 2024) says Switzerland has no AI-specific law and that the existing governance and risk-management rules apply. It names seven areas it checked: governance, inventory and risk classification, data quality, tests and ongoing monitoring, documentation, explainability, and independent review. Each area maps to an engineering artefact.
- The inventory has one row per use case, with an owner, the data sent, the pinned snapshot and its retirement date, the region, the allowed service credentials and a risk class. The class comes from a firm rule based on FINMA's proportionality factors (client impact, financial impact, whether a person checks the output) and decides the controls. A gateway service holds the provider keys, receives the use-case id, prompt version and end user from the authenticated caller, and refuses calls with no matching row. This keeps the inventory complete.
- Every call, in every class, writes an insert-only call row: use case, user and service, the model version the provider reports (OpenAI `model`, Gemini `modelVersion`), the prompt version (a git-versioned id), and pointers to the full prompt and the draft in an encrypted, access-controlled content store. Edits, approvals and deletions are later event rows keyed by call_id. The output is stored because it cannot be produced again. Lomem stamps its lesson notes with model and prompt version, but it records the configured model name and overwrites the draft on edit.
- Data residency and retention are provider contract terms to check and record per risk class. OpenAI, for example, keeps abuse-monitoring logs for up to 30 days by default, grants zero retention only with approval, and offers a 'Europe (EEA + Switzerland)' region, which does not promise Switzerland. The provider is managed as outsourcing under the third-party risk process, with an exit plan.
- Eval reports tied to a model version and prompt version are the validation evidence. They must be rerun when either changes, including a one-word prompt edit. Free text is scored by code checks, by a grader model calibrated against human labels, or by human reviewers. Calling a pinned snapshot rather than an alias keeps the evidence valid. Monitoring scores a weekly sample of live calls against the same bar and tracks the edit rate (draft vs final text) against thresholds. High-risk outputs wait in a pending state for a named human approver, and each release needs a reviewer from outside the building team.
- NIST AI RMF (four functions: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage; voluntary), its GenAI Profile AI 600-1, and the EU AI Act describe the same duties from other angles. Under the EU AI Act's risk tiers, creditworthiness and life or health insurance pricing are high-risk, with those duties applying from 2 Dec 2027.
